# Skalkaho Business Case

## Executive Summary

Skalkaho is a quote-building tool for small contractors who need to professionalize their estimating but aren't ready for enterprise construction software.

The construction software market has a gap: platforms like Buildxact and JobTread start at $170-200/month and require significant onboarding investment. Meanwhile, most small contractors—especially in rural and regional markets—use spreadsheets, paper, or gut instinct. They lose money on jobs because they can't track their true costs.

Skalkaho fills this gap with focused, affordable tooling that grows with the contractor. Starting price under $30/month, scaling to $150/month maximum as features expand. Never intended to compete with full construction management platforms—just serve the underserved.

**Pilot partner secured.** A Hamilton, MT contractor has committed to co-develop the MVP, providing real-world validation from day one.

## Problem Statement

### The Pain

Small contractors (1-10 employees) face a consistent problem: they win jobs but don't know if they made money until the job is done—sometimes not even then.

Root causes:

1. **Quoting is manual and error-prone.** Spreadsheets break. Formulas get overwritten. Copy-paste errors compound.

2. **Markup is inconsistent.** Some jobs get 15%, some get 25%, some get forgotten entirely. No system enforces consistency.

3. **Scope creep is invisible.** Changes happen on the job site but never make it back to the quote. Actual costs diverge from estimates with no visibility.

4. **"Software" means enterprise.** Tools that solve these problems cost $2,000-6,000/year and require weeks of onboarding. Overkill for a 3-person framing crew.

### The Result

Contractors leave money on the table. Industry data suggests 30-40% of small contractors operate on margins under 10%—often unknowingly. Many fail within 5 years, not from lack of work, but from lack of visibility into their numbers.

### Who Feels This Most

- Contractors doing $200K-$2M annual revenue
- 1-10 employees (often just owner + 1-3 crew)
- Residential remodels, additions, specialty trades
- Rural and regional markets where tech adoption lags
- Owner-operators who do sales, estimating, AND the work

## Market Analysis

### Market Size

**US Construction Industry (2024):**
- 3.5 million construction businesses
- 90%+ have fewer than 20 employees
- ~750,000 residential contractors (remodelers, home builders, specialty trades)

**Addressable Market:**
- Contractors doing $200K-$2M revenue: estimated 400,000-500,000 businesses
- Currently using spreadsheets/paper: estimated 60-70%
- "Software curious" but priced out: estimated 30-40% of those

**Conservative TAM:** 100,000-150,000 potential customers in the US alone.

### Competitive Landscape

| Tier | Examples | Price Point | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|------|----------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| Enterprise | Procore, Buildertrend | $500-2000+/mo | Full platform | Overkill, complex, expensive |
| Mid-Market | Buildxact, JobTread | $170-350/mo | Good features, growing | Still expensive, onboarding heavy |
| Budget | Joist, Invoice Simple | $15-50/mo | Affordable | Limited, often invoicing-focused |
| DIY | Excel, Google Sheets | Free | Familiar | Error-prone, no guardrails |

**The Gap:** No focused tool exists between "free but broken" and "$2,000/year commitment." Contractors either struggle with spreadsheets or make a major software investment they may not be ready for.

### Competitor Deep Dive

**Buildxact** (closest competitor positioning)
- Foundation tier: $169/mo annual ($2,030/year)
- Strong on takeoffs and estimating
- Unlimited users (good)
- AI features at higher tiers
- Heavy onboarding required
- Australian company, US expansion

**JobTread**
- $199/mo + $20/user ($2,400+/year for solo)
- All features included (no tier games)
- Strong on project management
- Dallas-based, good support reputation
- Still requires significant commitment

**Joist** (lower end)
- Free tier available
- $17-50/mo paid tiers
- Focused on invoicing, light estimating
- Mobile-first
- Less robust for complex quotes

## Target Customer Profile

### Primary Persona: "The Working Owner"

**Demographics:**
- Owner-operator of small contracting business
- 35-55 years old
- 5-20 years in the trade
- $300K-$1.5M annual revenue
- 1-5 employees (often family or long-term crew)
- Rural, suburban, or small-city market

**Psychographics:**
- Skilled tradesperson first, businessperson second
- Proud of their craft, frustrated by paperwork
- Skeptical of technology promises ("I've been burned before")
- Values relationships over transactions
- Word-of-mouth is primary marketing
- Would rather be on the job site than in front of a computer

**Current Behavior:**
- Quotes in Excel, Word, or on paper
- Markup is "gut feel" or flat percentage
- Tracks jobs in head or basic spreadsheet
- Often surprised by job profitability (good and bad)
- Has tried software before, found it too complex or expensive
- Spouse/partner may handle bookkeeping

**Trigger Events:**
- Lost money on a job they thought was profitable
- Spent a weekend redoing a quote because Excel broke
- Competitor won a bid they should have won
- Accountant/spouse asked "do you actually know your margins?"
- Considering hiring first employee, needs better systems

### Secondary Persona: "The New Pro"

**Demographics:**
- Recently started own business (1-3 years)
- 25-40 years old
- Came from working for larger contractor
- $100K-$500K annual revenue
- Solo or 1-2 employees

**Psychographics:**
- More tech-comfortable than older contractors
- Wants to "do it right from the start"
- Budget-conscious (startup phase)
- Hungry to grow but cautious about overhead

### Geographic Focus (Initial)

**Primary:** Mountain West / Northern Rockies
- Montana, Idaho, Wyoming, Eastern Washington/Oregon
- Smaller markets, underserved by tech
- Strong word-of-mouth networks
- Seasonal work patterns (good for software adoption in slow season)

**Why Here:**
- Founder familiarity (Hamilton, MT pilot)
- Less competition from enterprise sales teams
- Tight-knit contractor communities
- Lower customer acquisition cost via referral

## Value Proposition

### Core Promise

**"Know your numbers without the overhead."**

Skalkaho helps small contractors build accurate quotes and track real margins—without enterprise software complexity or cost.

### Key Benefits

1. **Quotes that calculate themselves.** Change a quantity, the total updates. No broken formulas, no copy-paste errors.

2. **Consistent markup, every job.** Set your surcharge once, apply it everywhere. Override when needed, inherit when not.

3. **See the real number.** Material cost, labor cost, markup, total—all visible, all the time.

4. **Start in minutes, not weeks.** No onboarding calls, no implementation fees, no training videos required.

5. **Price that makes sense.** Less than a tank of gas per month to start. Grows with you, never gouges you.

### What We Don't Do (Intentionally)

- We don't manage your whole business (use QuickBooks for accounting)
- We don't schedule your crews (use a calendar)
- We don't replace your phone (call your customers yourself)
- We don't pretend AI will estimate for you (you know your costs, we just organize them)

### Differentiation

| vs. Spreadsheets | vs. Buildxact/JobTread |
|------------------|------------------------|
| Structured data, not fragile formulas | 1/10th the cost to start |
| Built-in surcharge logic | No onboarding required |
| Can't accidentally break it | Focused tool, not a platform |
| Looks professional to customers | No feature overwhelm |

## Product Strategy

### Phase 1: MVP (Current Scope)

**Focus:** Quote building with real-time calculations

**Features:**
- Create jobs with customer info
- Organize line items into categories (nested)
- Material and labor item types
- Surcharge/markup at job, category, and line item level
- Stacking or override surcharge modes
- Real-time total calculations
- Responsive web interface (tablet-friendly)

**Not Included (Intentional):**
- User accounts / authentication
- Saving quotes to database
- PDF export
- Customer management
- Material lists
- Integrations

**Goal:** Validate core value prop with pilot customer. Can they build quotes faster and more accurately than their current method?

### Phase 2: Foundation

**Focus:** Make it a real product people can use daily

**Features:**
- User accounts and authentication
- Save and load quotes
- PDF export (professional quote documents)
- Quote templates (reusable starting points)
- Basic settings persistence

**Pricing:** Introduce paid tier ($15-25/month)

### Phase 3: Growth

**Focus:** Customer-facing professionalism and efficiency

**Features:**
- Customer database (contacts, history)
- Quote versioning (track revisions)
- Duplicate and modify quotes
- Email quotes directly
- Basic reporting (quotes sent, won/lost tracking)

**Pricing:** Add higher tier ($40-60/month)

### Phase 4: Expansion

**Focus:** Job lifecycle tracking

**Features:**
- Convert quote to job
- Track actual costs against estimate
- Change order management
- Material list generation
- Basic job profitability reporting

**Pricing:** Business tier ($100-150/month)

### Future Considerations

- QuickBooks integration
- Supplier pricing feeds
- Mobile app
- Multi-user / team features
- Photo documentation

**Guiding Principle:** Add features only when customers ask for them AND they align with the core mission of "know your numbers." Resist platform creep.

## Pricing Strategy

### Philosophy

- **Accessible entry point.** First tier should feel like "why not try it?"
- **Value-based tiers.** Each tier adds meaningful capability, not artificial limits.
- **No per-user gouging.** Small teams shouldn't be penalized.
- **Predictable costs.** No surprise fees, no usage charges.
- **Fair growth.** Prices scale with value delivered, not with lock-in.

### Proposed Tier Structure

| Tier | Price | Target User | Core Value |
|------|-------|-------------|------------|
| **Starter** | $19/mo | Solo contractor, getting started | Build unlimited quotes, basic features |
| **Pro** | $49/mo | Established contractor, growing | PDF export, templates, customer database |
| **Business** | $99-149/mo | Small team, full workflow | Job tracking, reporting, multi-user |

**Annual discount:** 15-20% (2 months free equivalent)

### Competitive Positioning

| | Skalkaho Starter | Joist Pro | Buildxact Foundation | JobTread |
|-|------------------|-----------|---------------------|----------|
| Monthly | $19 | $50 | $169 | $199+ |
| Annual | ~$190 | ~$500 | ~$2,030 | ~$2,400+ |

**Message:** "Real quoting software for less than your streaming subscriptions."

### Pilot Pricing

For pilot customer:
- Free during MVP development
- Discounted first year at launch ($99/year?)
- Feedback and testimonial commitment

## Risks and Mitigations

### Risk: Limited market size

**Concern:** Niche may be too small for meaningful revenue.

**Mitigation:** 
- Validate with pilot before heavy investment
- Expand geographic focus if regional model works
- Adjacent markets (handymen, landscapers, trades)

### Risk: Competitors move downmarket

**Concern:** Buildxact or JobTread launch budget tiers.

**Mitigation:**
- Speed to market in underserved segment
- Build loyalty through community focus
- Stay lean, don't need venture-scale returns
- Regional relationships harder to replicate

### Risk: Customers outgrow the product

**Concern:** Successful customers graduate to larger platforms.

**Mitigation:**
- This is partially expected and okay
- Build upgrade path within Skalkaho (Business tier)
- Focus on new customer acquisition
- Some graduation = healthy market validation

### Risk: Feature creep / scope expansion

**Concern:** Pressure to become "full platform" dilutes focus.

**Mitigation:**
- Document what we don't do (and why)
- Feature requests require multiple customer validation
- Maintain "tool not platform" positioning
- Integrations over rebuilding (QuickBooks for accounting, etc.)

### Risk: Technology dependency

**Concern:** Solo/small dev team, bus factor risk.

**Mitigation:**
- Simple, maintainable technology choices
- Good documentation
- Avoid over-engineering
- Consider open-sourcing core components
